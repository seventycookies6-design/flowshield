## Method

Read the root and DesktopApp rules, `VM_TESTING.md`, the lab control surface and guest helpers, the previous round-4 report, and the relevant app UI/settings/update code. All VM actions used `powershell.exe -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File C:\HyperV\FlowShield-Lab\Scripts\lab.ps1` through a transcript-recording host wrapper. One bridge call ran at a time.

Runner files were written on the host outside the repo, then transferred with `stage`. Installer launches, app launches, UI Automation and guest input ran through a Limited interactive scheduled task; PS Direct performed filesystem/event-log probes. Screenshots used the lab's guest interactive capture task. No host desktop, host UI Automation, host input, host screenshot, host app launch, pytest or dotnet build was used.

Installers ran with `--silent`. Terms were accepted by invoking the real `AcceptTermsButton`, then `FirstRunSkipButton`. To quit fully, the runner turned **Minimize to tray on close** off through the Settings toggle and closed the app through its WindowPattern; it waited for zero FlowShield processes. Settings were produced by the app, never seeded or edited by the runner. Their protected envelopes were copied, decrypted under the guest user solely for selected safe summaries and per-property hashes, and compared across launches/update.

The 1.0.91 x64 installer, full package, `RELEASES`, `releases.win.json` and `assets.win.json` were staged as an archive and expanded to **`C:\feed`**. Only the relaunched guest process and its updater descendants received **`FLOWSHIELD_UPDATE_FEED=C:\feed`**; no persistent environment setting was created. Settings → Check for updates and Restart to update were both invoked through their real enabled UI controls.

Every wait had a deadline: control waits 45–120 s, installer 180 s, exit 30 s, observation loop 80 s, bridge completion probes 20 s, scheduled task ceiling 8 min, and independent host process ceilings 150 s (380 s for revert). Bridge requests also carried explicit 35–90 s timeouts, apart from revert's built-in bounded readiness loop. Sustained-runtime observations sampled the same PID every five seconds for at least 65 s. Crash checks inspected all **Application Error** and **.NET Runtime** provider events in the guest Application log since that check's boot.
